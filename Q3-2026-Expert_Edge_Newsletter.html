03 · Interview with an Expert
A change story
Michelle Bain
“Often, helping people see the story is what enables them to move forward with confidence.”
Michelle Bain
You moved onto DigiMod in July when things were still ambiguous. What was that first stretch like?

The first few weeks were about becoming comfortable with uncertainty. There were lots of ideas, assumptions and opportunities being discussed, but not always a shared understanding of how they connected. Rather than trying to define everything immediately, I focused on listening, asking questions and understanding different perspectives.

What I found was that people often understood their own part of the picture but were struggling to see how it related to the broader programme. That's where I started using visual models and diagrams to bring conversations together. Creating a shared picture helped uncover gaps, challenge assumptions and move discussions forward much faster than lengthy documents alone.

A lot of your early work was building visual stories of what we are trying to achieve to help stakeholders align during discovery. How did you figure out where to start when so much was still unclear?

When things are unclear, I find it helpful to start by capturing what we do know rather than worrying about what we don't. I begin by listening to stakeholders, identifying recurring themes and looking for connections between people, processes, decisions and outcomes.

As a visual storyteller, I use graphics and mind maps to make those connections visible. A diagram doesn't just document information, it helps tell the story of how the programme works today, where the pain points are, and where opportunities exist. Once people can see the story laid out visually, it becomes much easier to align on priorities and identify the next steps.

What would you tell another Expert who's nervous about stepping into a new team, project or programme?

Don't feel pressured to have all the answers straight away. Some of the most valuable contributions come from asking good questions and helping others make sense of complexity.

New environments can feel overwhelming, especially when they're evolving quickly, but that's often where our skills add the most value. Be curious, spend time understanding different perspectives and don't underestimate the impact of turning complex discussions into something clear and visual. Often, helping people see the story is what enables them to move forward with confidence.

We're simplifying the new-starter experience — moving away from one overloaded email and towards earlier, clearer communication at each stage: contract-signing, pre-start and post-start. We're also making sure there's clear ownership and continuity throughout.
Timesheets — weekly, please
Please submit your timesheet each week. To make sure you're paid on time, it's worth checking your timesheets are approved by month end — we can't include anything still awaiting approval in that month's pay, so a quick nudge to your approver goes a long way.
Labour Day — Monday 26 October
A reminder that Labour Day is a public holiday in New Zealand. There's nothing to log for the day, so please leave it blank on your timesheet (no need to enter a zero), and reach out to your ERM if you have any questions.
Planning leave and the Christmas break
With the end of the year approaching, now's a good time to start thinking about your leave. Many clients slow down or shut down over the Christmas and New Year period, so please let us know about any leave you're taking or planning to take — including time around client shutdowns. It helps us keep you, your client and your timesheets on the same page. Whether it's a day or a few weeks, just let your ERM know as soon as you have your dates.

07 · Talent market update
What our Talent team is seeing

A quarterly read on the market from the team closest to it — what's shifting for candidates, clients and contract work across NZ.

New Zealand Market Update
From Amy Walker, Head of Talent & Supply, NZ
Market Overview

The NZ technology and business transformation market is still relatively buoyant this close to election. SEEK job ad volume is up almost 10% on this time last year and still climbing. Across the board recruitment processes are taking longer as many of the strongest candidates remain employed in permanent positions or have secured contract extensions. This has reduced the immediate availability of high-calibre technology resources and extended time-to-fill across a number of specialist roles.

Where is the demand?
  • Project management: increasing. We're seeing more demand for Project Managers across the board, including SAP Project Managers.
  • Architecture: one of the busier segments of the market currently, with organisations seeking capability across enterprise, solution and cloud architecture disciplines as transformation programmes progress.
  • Data-focused roles; also continue to attract demand, reflecting ongoing investment in data capability, reporting, analytics and AI-related initiatives.
  • Business analysis: remains steady and consistent, with organisations continuing to engage experienced BAs across technology delivery, transformation and business change programmes. Demand levels have remained relatively stable throughout the year.
  • AI - 4.0% of NZ job ads now reference AI skills, according to SEEK. Mentions rose 4.4% month on month and 93.3% year on year - nearly doubling in 12 months. The most commonly requested AI skills:
    • Agentic AI: up 7.1% month on month
    • AI Ethics & Governance: up 7.9% month on month
    • Generative AI: up 2.3% month on month

This indicates organisations are moving beyond experimentation and increasingly focusing on AI governance, responsible implementation and practical deployment of AI-centric solutions.

Job Ad Volume

SEEK data shows New Zealand job ad volume up almost 10% on this time last year, and steadily increasing. Volumes are recovering from the 2024–25 low, though still below the 2016 average and short of the 2022 peak.

08 · Inside Expert360
An interview with
Evan
Setu Evan Belford · Practice Director

As a Practice Director, Evan works directly with clients across the Wellington and Auckland tech and transformation markets. We asked him for his read on Q3 from the coalface — what's shifted, and what to watch heading into Q4.

Has anything shifted in your corner of the market this quarter that's changed how you're working with clients?
Auckland and Wellington are moving at different speeds right now. Auckland's quieter, but there's genuine improvement in confidence and hiring activity — we're also seeing our financial services and banking clients lean more toward an Auckland-based workforce. Wellington's more mixed: some public sector clients are active with new projects and need extra hands to help close out work already in flight, while others are still in freeze mode or working through change. Budget pressure and a lack of senior candidates are the two biggest hurdles coming up in almost every client conversation at the moment. It's also worth being upfront about rates — cost of living is understandably pushing a lot of you to ask for more, but clients are feeling the same cost pressure, so there can be a gap between the ask and what's on the table. Worth factoring in as you think about negotiating.
What are you expecting to see more of in your division heading into Q4?
A few things worth keeping an eye on. Election season in November could see a bit of a slowdown in hiring in the lead-up, as agencies hold off on decisions — even though there's plenty of talk about work that still needs doing. More broadly, expect hiring to stay a little slower and more cautious than usual — if a process feels slow, that's the market being careful, not a reflection on you. And on AI: clients see it as a way to bring costs down over time, but it still takes skilled people to actually build and implement it. If that's your space, the opportunity is very much still there — it's just shifting toward who can deliver the change, not just talk about it.
